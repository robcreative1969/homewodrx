  text; the site uses HomeWodRX (347 times), HomeWODrx (190) and HomeWodRx (1).
- Speak to the athlete as "you". Short sentences. Say the useful thing first.
- Write for the visitor, never for ourselves. Example to fix in the shop: "one of the
  most effective tools you can recommend".
- No fitness myths. Example to fix: "flush out lactic acid" on stretch routines.
- Say "the team at HomeWODRx" for bylines (blog posts now say "By the HomeWodRX team").

Specific changes:
- Rename **"Time Cap"** to **"Typical time"** when the number is an expected finish

  ones that only need a pull-up bar. They should match the equipment and the workout.

## 5. Numbers

- Use real counts, pulled from the database: **204 workouts, 226 movements, 54 stretches**
  today. Never "Hundreds", "200+" or "50+".
- The workout list should count benchmark workouts and The Daily 20 separately (today it
  says "Showing 294" while the header says 204 and the page description says 107).
- The Daily 20 and The Daily 10 must be the same on the home page and /daily-wod. On
  October 4 they showed different workouts while both said "same for all visitors".

## 6. Formatting

- Ranges use an en dash with no spaces: **2–8 min**, **15–30 reps**.
- Weights: **95 lb / 65 lb** (men's first). Heights: **24 in / 20 in**.
- Distances: **400 m Run**, **1,000 m Row**. No rep counts in front of single efforts
  (today: "1 · 1 Mile Run").
- Coaching cues are full sentences ending with a period.
- Tags and chips in Title Case: Quadriceps, Glutes, Hamstrings.
- One link style for "read more": **Read →**.
- Never show raw database values or loading text to visitors (today: "CLASSIC_BENCHMARK"
  in search, "Verifying security…" on signup).

## 7. Words to explain

Beginners meet these words without explanation. Explain each the first time it appears
on a page (a short tooltip), and add a **Glossary** page that they all link to.

Rx, Scaled, AMRAP, EMOM, For Time, Tabata, Chipper, Time cap, Kipping, C2B (chest-to-bar),
T2B (toes-to-bar), Pood, PNF, Bilateral.

Also fix the misuse of "Bilateral": today a stretch says "Bilateral: Both Sides" next
to "Perform on each side".
